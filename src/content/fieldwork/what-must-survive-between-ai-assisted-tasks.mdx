Before a later AI-assisted task reuses earlier work, it needs to recover the conditions that make reuse safe—not simply receive the last answer.
A useful output can complete one task. It does not tell a later task what remains usable.
The work may have changed since the earlier output. A source may have been rechecked, a decision revised, implementation advanced, a claim narrowed or an uncertainty left open.
The practical question is therefore not only:
> What was said?
It is:
> What may still be used for this task, and on what basis?
<aside class="article-callout"><strong>DIRECT ANSWER</strong><p>A later AI-assisted task can continue responsibly only when it recovers what it is continuing, what supports it, why the direction still applies, what has changed, and who can authorise reuse or change.</p></aside>
## The five handoff questions
Before continuing work that may affect a claim, decision or current state, use this lightweight handoff. The questions identify the context relevant to the task; they do not require every task to carry every record.
<table>
<tr>
<th scope="col">Ask</th>
<th scope="col">Recover</th>
<th scope="col">If it is unclear</th>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>1. What exactly are we continuing?</td>
<td>The current task, intended decision or bounded claim.</td>
<td>Restate or narrow the task before reusing prior work.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>2. What supports it?</td>
<td>The relevant source and evidence path.</td>
<td>Recheck the source, investigate further or reduce the claim.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>3. Why does this direction still apply?</td>
<td>The decision, rationale and known alternatives.</td>
<td>Confirm that the decision still applies, or reopen it; do not treat history as authority.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>4. What is current, superseded or unresolved?</td>
<td>Current state, changed conditions and visible limitations.</td>
<td>Pause, mark the uncertainty or ask for a current-state check.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>5. Who can authorise reuse or change?</td>
<td>The accountable human role and the decision it can make.</td>
<td>Escalate; do not convert an unapproved inference into an operating conclusion.</td>
</tr>
</table>
The table is deliberately small. Its purpose is not to create another large governance process. Its purpose is to interrupt the assumption that a previous answer is automatically ready for reuse.
## The signal to look for
A later task should not begin with:
> Here is the previous output. Continue from this.
It should begin with:
> Here is the previous output, its supporting context, its current status, its limitations and the authority under which it may be reused.
That distinction changes how AI-based applications should be designed.
The handoff is not only a prompt-writing concern. It is a system-boundary concern.
If the next task cannot recover what it is continuing, what supports it, what changed and who can authorise the next move, then the system is not truly continuing work. It is reconstructing work from incomplete memory.
## How the handoff changes the next action
The handoff does not produce one automatic outcome. It makes the appropriate next action visible.
Sometimes earlier material can be reused. Sometimes the claim must be narrowed. Sometimes the source must be checked again. Sometimes the previous decision must be reopened. Sometimes the task must pause because the required authority is absent.
The important result is not always a completed answer. It may be a visible insufficiency, a review state or an escalation request.
That is especially important for agentic systems. An agent that can continue acting must also know when continuation is not justified.
## What this looked like inside the Inspiral agentic flow
The handoff problem became visible while reviewing the connected implementation stages of the Inspiral agentic flow.
STG-02, STG-03, STG-04, STG-05 and STG-06 are not isolated AI experiments. They form a sequence in which context, semantic meaning, evidence, model outputs and implementation state must move safely from one stage to the next.
The review examined whether each stage preserved:
- governed source material;
- semantic profiles and capability meaning;
- task-specific model requests;
- typed outputs and provider status;
- evidence and authority references;
- review-required conditions;
- persistence and downstream reconstruction.
The review therefore examined the complete AI boundary:
- what entered the OpenAI request;
- which task and output contract were active;
- which adapter and execution path were used;
- whether incomplete or failed provider responses were preserved;
- whether model references matched permitted authority references;
- whether review-required conditions survived into later stages;
- whether the result could be reconciled deterministically.
The review confirmed that STG-06 could reuse the existing OpenAI adapter already used by earlier stages in the Inspiral flow. No new adapter or orchestration framework was required. The public-safe implementation summary is recorded in the approved LCP-02 evidence record.
It also found that the flow could not yet safely proceed. Unresolved issues included the active repository path, exhaustive request budgets, Batch recovery, output and transport measurement, persistence and downstream intake.

The important lesson was not simply that one stage had encountered errors. It was that an agentic flow must carry forward failure states, limitations and review conditions rather than allowing the next stage to treat an incomplete result as ready.
## What earlier stages revealed
The connected stages of the Inspiral agentic flow provided concrete examples of what must survive between tasks.
- **STG-02** showed why terminal provider output must not be silently discarded.
- **STG-03** showed why output capacity must reflect the actual schema rather than an assumed response size.
- **STG-05** showed why large persistence payloads may require bounded reference transport.
- **STG-06** showed why the entire AI boundary must be reviewed, including input construction, execution, output handling, authority validation and review propagation.
These are not isolated implementation details. They are handoff conditions within the flow.
If they disappear between stages, the next stage may receive an apparently valid result without knowing how trustworthy, complete or reusable it is.
## A handoff is more than passing data
Passing data from one task to another is not the same as passing understanding.
A later task may receive the previous result but still lack:
- the source from which it came;
- the evidence supporting it;
- the decision that shaped it;
- the state in which it was produced;
- the limitations that constrained it;
- the authority required to change it.
Without those elements, the later task may produce a fluent continuation while silently changing the meaning of the earlier work.
The five handoff questions create a small control surface between tasks. They help preserve not only the answer, but also the conditions under which the answer may be trusted.

## When this method is insufficient
The five questions are not a substitute for evidence, current-state checks or human judgement.
They cannot:
- establish an unsupported relationship;
- make an obsolete record current;
- resolve contradictory authority;
- approve a consequential change;
- turn an incomplete model result into a complete one;
- guarantee that a technical implementation is production-ready.
When the material is incomplete, conflicting or too sensitive for inference, the correct result may be to hold, investigate or escalate.
## The boundary for agentic work
An agentic solution should not be designed only around:
> Input → model → output
It should also account for:
> Context → task → model interaction → result → validation → authority → next task
The durable unit is not the last answer. It is the answer together with its provenance, decision context, current state, limitations, review status and permission to continue.
That is what must survive between AI-assisted tasks.

This Method note draws on the LCP-02 handoff analysis and the STG-02–STG-06 implementation review conducted within the Inspiral agentic flow.
